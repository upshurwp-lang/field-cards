a6700 Field Cards

Kit: a6700 · 15mm f/1.4 G · Sigma 30mm f/1.4 · 18-135 · 70-350 G · 55mm polarizer · Peak Design tripod

Do this once at home. Then each card is a single turn of the mode dial.

Every card assumes

  • RAW + JPEG. RAW lets you rescue exposure later. The JPEG is for quick sharing on the iPad.
  • Auto ISO with a ceiling of 6400 by day and 12800 at night. The a6700 cleans up well to 6400.
  • Mechanical shutter. The electronic shutter bends fast-moving subjects (rolling shutter).
  • Histogram on in the viewfinder display. Trust it over the screen.
  • Format the card in camera at the start of each trip, never on the iPad.

Lens buttons

The round button on the side of the 70-350 and the 15mm is Focus Hold. By default, holding it freezes autofocus. You can reassign it: MENU → Setup → Operation Customize → Custom Key/Dial Setting → Focus Hold Button. Menu wording may differ slightly by firmware.

P mode (Program): the grab-and-go setting

The camera picks shutter speed and aperture. You still control ISO, exposure comp, focus, burst and white balance. Use it between sightings, around camp, for people and landscapes, and with the 18-135.

  1. Mode dial to P. Half-press: the screen shows the camera's pick, e.g. 1/250 F5.6.
  2. One-time setup: MENU → Exposure → ISO AUTO Min. SS → Faster. This keeps P from choosing shutter speeds too slow for the 70-350.
  3. Program Shift: turn the front or rear dial while metering. P becomes P*. One way gives a faster shutter (freeze motion), the other a higher f-number (more in focus). It resets when you change mode or power off.
  4. Exposure comp via Fn → Exposure Comp. Same rules as the cards: minus for bright or white, plus for dark or backlit.

P doesn't know your subject is moving. When an animal might run, turn to Dial 1. When a bird takes off, Dial 2 or the 70-350's lens button.

Exposure in one line each

Shutter speed
Freezes or blurs motion. The first thing to set for animals.
Aperture (f-number)
Low number lets in more light and blurs the background. High number gives more depth in focus.
ISO
Brightens the image after the fact. Costs noise. Let Auto ISO handle it.
Exposure comp (±)
Works in M with Auto ISO. Minus for bright scenes or white animals. Plus for dark animals or backlight.

Use two or more animals at f/8–f/11 so both are sharp. A single animal stays wide open.

Technique

  • Ask the driver to turn off the engine. Idle vibration shows at 350mm.
  • SteadyShot on. Brace on the window frame or roll bar. Never rest the lens on the vehicle while the engine runs.
  • Burst Mid (about 8 fps) for animals that are mostly still. It saves card space and culling time.
  • Focus on the eye. If the eye is soft, the shot is soft.
  • Leave space in the direction the animal faces.
  • Shoot from a low seat when you can. Eye level beats looking down.

Hi+ is 11 fps. The buffer fills in a few seconds, so shoot in short bursts.

Technique

  • Find the bird at about 200mm first, then zoom in. At 350mm it's hard to find in the frame.
  • Keep both eyes open if you can, so you see the bird coming before it enters the frame.
  • Half-press early and hold it so tracking locks on before the moment.
  • Pan from your hips, not your arms, and keep following through after the burst.
  • On a perched bird, frame loose and wait for takeoff. Birds usually lean forward and poop just before they leave.
  • Blue sky behind a dark bird underexposes it. Dial +0.7 to +1.

Polarizer (55mm: fits the 15mm and 18-135)

  • Strongest at 90° from the sun. Point at the sun with one arm and the other arm shows the best direction.
  • Rotate it while watching the screen. Stop when the water turns clear and turquoise.
  • On the 15mm, a polarizer can darken one side of the sky more than the other. Ease off or take it off if the sky looks blotchy.
  • It costs about 1.5 stops. Take it off at dusk.

Technique

  • Expose for the sky. A bright sky clips first, and dark ground recovers better in RAW.
  • High contrast: bracket 3 frames at ±1.3 EV and merge later.
  • Turn on the grid and electronic level. A tilted horizon is the easiest mistake to avoid.
  • Give it a foreground: a termite mound, a granite boulder, tracks in sand.
  • Telephoto compresses distance. A setting sun at 350mm looks enormous behind trees.
  • Don't aim at the sun at long focal lengths for long when it's high. It can damage the sensor. Low sun at sunset is fine.

f/1.4 looks dreamy, but the depth in focus is very thin. One eye sharp, the other soft. Start at f/2 until you trust it.

Light

  • Open shade beats direct sun: under a tent awning, on the shaded side of the lodge.
  • Sun behind the person gives hair glow. Dial +0.7 to +1 so the face isn't dark.
  • Midday sun on faces makes harsh shadows. Turn them so the sun is at their back, or move into shade.
  • Golden hour on a lodge deck is the portrait you'll want to frame.

With people you don't know

  • Always ask first. On a Maasai village visit, ask your guide what's expected. Payment or a community contribution is common and usually arranged through the lodge.
  • For children, ask a parent.
  • Show them the shot on the screen afterward. It turns the moment into a conversation.
  • Guides and camp staff are usually happy to be photographed with the vehicle or the view. Ask anyway.

Dial 3. The screen turns red to protect your night vision. Turn phone brightness all the way down.

Set up before dark

  1. M mode, lens switch to MF. SteadyShot off on the tripod.
  2. Drive: 2-sec self-timer.
  3. White balance 4000K manual.
  4. Long Exposure NR off. Otherwise each frame takes twice as long.
  5. Monitor brightness manual, low. Judge exposure on the histogram.
  6. Remove the UV filter. It adds flare and halos around bright stars.

Nail focus

  1. The 15mm's focus ring has no hard stop at infinity. Turning it all the way is not sharp.
  2. Aim at the brightest star. Turn on Focus Magnifier at full zoom.
  3. Turn the ring until the star is the smallest point. Then leave it alone.
  4. Take a test shot and zoom in on playback. You want a dot, not a donut.

Mara night drives

  • Handheld. SteadyShot on. Brace and ask for the engine off.
  • Spotlit animals: 70-350, f/5.6–6.3, 1/160–1/250, Auto ISO capped at 12800, Animal eye AF.
  • Closer or wider scenes: 15mm f/1.4 at 1/60 keeps ISO low.
  • Spot metering on the animal, –0.7 to –1 EV. Shoot bursts and keep the sharpest frame.

iPhone in a SeaTouch housing with UWACAM. Read this on the boat, not at depth.

On the boat, before you roll in

  1. Charge to 100%. Turn on Airplane mode and set Auto-Lock to Never so the screen doesn't lock mid-dive.
  2. Clean the O-ring and check it for hair or sand. One grain is enough to flood it.
  3. Dunk test the empty housing in the rinse tank, then put the phone in.
  4. Open UWACAM, check UWACOLOR is on, and take a test shot on deck.
  5. Close the housing in shade or AC. Sealing in warm humid air fogs the port.

Which mode

AUTO
Start here for the first dives. Big buttons, UWACOLOR fixes color for you. Pick 1× or 0.5×.
PRO
Once AUTO feels easy. Manual exposure, ISO and white balance, plus histogram, zebras (blown highlights) and focus peaking.
Grey card (PRO)
Point at a white slate, sand or your palm and tap Grey Card. Redo it every ~5 m of depth change. Color shifts fast with depth.

Skip the telephoto lens underwater. Zooming in puts more water between you and the subject, which costs contrast and color. Move closer instead.

Technique

  • Get close, then closer. Under 1 m is ideal. Water strips out red within about 5 m.
  • Shallow water (5–12 m) on a sunny day gives the best natural color.
  • Get low and shoot slightly up at fish. Blue water behind beats a busy reef.
  • Flip to the upside-down UI for low-angle shots near the sand.
  • Hold still and exhale before you press. Buoyancy first. Never kneel on or touch the reef for a shot.
  • Give turtles space and never block their path to the surface.
  • Snorkeling: try half-in, half-out split shots in calm water. Wipe the port first.

After the dive

  • Soak the closed housing in fresh water. Press every button a few times to flush salt.
  • Towel dry, especially around the seal, before opening.
  • Batch-apply UWACOLOR to the keepers at the lodge. AirDrop to the iPad for backup.
